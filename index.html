How the ramp gets approached

Learn the product and the market fast

The expectation would be applying the same research-driven habits developed in real estate to shorten the ramp: learning how the product is positioned and priced, and identifying decision makers and key players in the market.

Presenting is almost a daily occurrence

Currently pitching for new business at least three times a week. Establishing rapport and building trust with clients always comes first. Then the services or product on offer and the competitive advantages it delivers, as well as the company's unique positioning. About half of these presentations run over Microsoft Teams, for vendors based anywhere in the world. Winning the business starts by creating a good connection, as a first step to putting a deal together.

Teamwork is an integral part of success

Currently operating within a very close-knit team of four at Jellis Craig. About half of the business we generate is run in collaboration with one another, negotiation and client management included. Working well within a team is a competitive advantage in itself, a way to combine strengths and get more wins together.

Full-Cycle Deal Ownership

Every deal is really two sales. Step one: an inbound or outbound lead, qualifying the opportunity, pitching, and winning the mandate (listing). Step two: going to market, meeting and qualifying the buyer, then negotiating and closing the deal, almost exclusively done remotely by phone, FaceTime, or Teams. The one exception is auction day, where it happens live. Each stage feeds the next, one person accountable from first contact to signed deal.

Multi-Channel Pipeline Generation

Pipeline hygiene runs on three layers. First, tiered cadence: every contact is segmented by engagement, warm contacts followed up every one to two weeks, mid-tier quarterly, dormant contacts twice a year, so nothing goes cold by accident. Second, signal-based outreach: market developments relevant to a specific contact, plus known personal or business events, trigger a touchpoint outside the regular cadence. Third, network maintenance: monthly contact with the referral and industry network, and twice-yearly (or more, depending on circumstance) check-ins with past clients, keeping every relationship live long after the deal closes.

Consultative, Trusted-Advisor Selling

Not every qualified opportunity should be closed immediately. Reading a client's actual circumstances, timing, budget, competing priorities, means sometimes the right advice is to wait for the right moment rather than push for a signature. Trust earned that way is what keeps clients coming back and referring others instead of being sold to once.

Full-Cycle Velocity

Speed comes from three habits:

  • Response time. Inbound leads answered in under an hour, inquiries answered within 24 hours.
  • Due diligence. Accurate pricing and full market research done upfront, so positioning and targeting are right the first time instead of corrected mid-cycle.
  • Standardized systems. The process is known ahead of time, and collateral is ready in advance, ahead of need rather than produced reactively once a deal is already moving.
